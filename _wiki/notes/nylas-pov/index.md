The rain had picked up by the time Nyla separated from the rest of the party, leaving Brush and Urgo to return to the Lucky Leap tavern by themselves. It would be fine, she told herself, these two may have no sense of direction, but the tavern was more or less just down the street, and she had explained the way as best as she could. If only Leon had been with them, she might have been feeling a bit less uneasy, but he had snuck off somewhere. 
Yawning as she made her way through the rain, she tried not to fall asleep, putting one foot in front of the other, even though she had to use all her focus to stop herself from stumbling on the uneven cobblestone streets. 
There was a faint smell of smoke wafting through the air, but Nyla ignored it, not wanting to be reminded of the hearthfires that must be burning in the houses surrounding her, families surrounding them as they welcomed the evening, huddled together for comfort against the darkness that she had to sneak through, like a thief in the night. 
As always, Rozena floated next to her, unusually quiet ever since they left the Vegabond hideout. Nyla wanted to ask her what had surprised her friend so, but couldn't find the energy to speak up. Maybe it had been a bad idea after all to go now, but last night she had made the decision to visit the temple and look after the priests and temple servants, the people she had slowly started to see as family over the last... one, no it had been almost two years now, hadn't it? 
As she thought back, Nyla realized that perhaps this was the first decision she had made since then, since the day she decided to die, so it felt almost good to finally do something, simply because she wanted to, even if it was just to take back a little bit of control. She had just been going with the flow since that day, staying at the temple because it was convenient... No, Nyla shook her head slowly. Perhaps that had simply been an excuse. 
True, her service to the various gods had started as a debt to repay, but over time, Nyla had grown fond of that life. Before, she never had the luxury to worry for others, always on the brink of starvation, constantly vigilant for the people around her who would not hesitate to steal her food, beat her up if she resisted, or simply because they felt like it. 
But now Nyla realized that she had truly come to cherish the opportunity to do good for the people who came to the temples looking for healing, help, or simply an ear to listen to their story. She had just been too shy, too afraid of the change, to admit it, instead using her debt as a pretense. 
Even tired as she was, Nyla chuckled quietly to herself. How silly this all was. But maybe, just maybe... Once this was all over, she could finally take it seriously and actually learn with the other adepts who would go to Mia's classes that Nyla herself often skipped. The thought of receiving the sash that marked a bonafide priestess still seemed strange, but somehow also comforting. A future to work towards. 
Nyla found herself speeding up as she thought, walking briskly through the rain while pulling her hood deep into her face. Normally she'd curse out the constant drops battering her from the sky, but now even that seemed little more than a nuisance. Nothing compared to her growing desire to come home, and spend at least one evening laughing, eating, drinking with everyone during dinner, before she would have to return to the mountainous task laid in front of her. 
Noting that the burnt smell that had been lingering was growing more faint, Nyla turned a corner and almost ran into Rozena who was floating in front of her with her back turned.
"W-whoa, what are you doing?" Nyla asked as she took a step back to find her balance again, quickly letting her fingers run underneath the raincloak to make sure it had not been disheveled from the sudden motion. Yet when her friend didn't answer, Nyla, feeling something was wrong, hesitantly reached out towards the wine-red cloak that she knew she could never reach. 
"Hey, are you alri-" The words got stuck in her throat when Rozena finally turned around, her eyes wide and glancing around, avoiding her own. "Oh, you almost scared me, Rozena..." Nyla spoke up, clearing her throat from the unexplainable lump she suddenly felt, and letting out a nervous laugh. "I didn't take you for the type to be easily spooked... get it?... It's funny..."
In response, Rozena silently floated down to her level, her blood red eyes still cast down as she laid her hands on Nyla's shoulders, the sensation of touch like always more in her own mind than actually there.
"...Don't...", came a whispered voice from beyond the waterfall of silky black hair that fell to the sides of Rozena's face, covering more than half of it.
"What did you say?", Nyla asked, her own voice sinking as she felt her heart drop with a terrible premonition that made the blood in her veins freeze. "Rozena, what do you mean?" Her voice now came louder, more desperate, from her mouth. "What is going o-"
"Please!" The sudden forcefulness in her friend's voice made Nyla shrink back reflexively, and she squinted her eyes in shock. When she turned them towards Rozena again, the woman had raised her head, her familiar blood red eyes now fixed on Nyla's own with a desperate intensity she had not seen often in her.
"Just tell me what is over... there..." Nyla tried speaking up again, but her voice trailed off when she saw her friend's uncertain expression, the slight quiver of her lips, the way she tried to lock Nyla's gaze on her eyes to keep them from wandering to far, while clearly being uncertain herself. 
"...Don't go there... please..." The words now came in a whisper, her voice gentle and caring like Nyla was used to, but with an almost begging undertone. The stark difference to before just made Nyla more uneasy. "What do you mean? We have to go through here to get to the temple, it's the only way..." Nyla stretched her neck to the side to look past Rozena while she spoke.
And her world dulled.

Barely visible through the heavy downpour, countless figures wearing the bright robes of priesthood had been strung up underneath the stone bridge spanning the river, its previously gentle waters now dark and thrashing violently in the same wind that made the bodies sway gently, like a horribly twisted approximation of a white wisteria tree.
Within a single moment, all color seemed to vanish from Nyla's vision, as she stumbled backwards against the wall. She felt as if a horse had stomped her chest, leaving her breathless and gasping for air, while her eyes widened, forcing the sight deep into her mind. In an effort not to scream, she covered her mouth with a shaking hand, but it would have been in vain anyway. 
No sound was coming her lips besides a garbled "N-no...", which was quickly swallowed by the overpowering noise of rain, only audible to Rozena who looked at her with a pained expression, as she felt her own inability to offer Nyla the right comfort sting her soul like needles. 
"I... I am so sorry," she finally whispered, but for once the words did not reach Nyla, as her ears began to ring with a high-pitched tone that drowned out even the rain.
Suddenly, Nyla found herself on the ground, registering only in a distant part of her mind that her knees must have given out underneath her, while feeling her clothes get soaked with the accursed rainwater covering the street in puddles.
Why... why were the robes hanging from the bridge? Nyla asked herself slowly, each word passing through her mind slowly, as if pushed through viscuous honey. Rotten, bitter honey that threatened to seep into every fiber of her being and drown her underneath.
"Nyla, we should go. It's not safe here." A voice nuzzled against her, distant and muffled.
No, that was wrong... they weren't robes, they were people wearing robes. Priests, temple servants, clerics in the colors of all faiths.
"You have to stand up Nyla, please!" The voice grew more insistent, but it did not make sense.
Why would someone hang themselves from a bridge like that? That was dangerous, wasn't it?
Something blocked Nyla's view, and it took her a moment to realize it was her own hand, reaching out into the distance, as if trying to push the little specks of white surrounded by the unforgiving darkness upwards, back on top of the bridge where they would be safe. The rainwater ran in streams down her fingers, dropping to the ground and flowing into her sleeves, just like the raindrops that got blown underneath Nyla's hood were covering her unblinking eyes, blurring her vision.
The faint image of her friend, a swirl of red and black was still shouting at her, the voice slowly growing louder and more desperate, while faded hands tried to pull her up and away.
That was wrong though, wasn't it? Right now, she shouldn't run away, not when her family was in danger. Right now she should-
"Help them...", Nyla whispered, crawling on all fours towards the distant bridge. "I have to help them! Or... or... or they'll die!"
Stumbling to her feet, she first started walking, then jogging, until she ran at full force, her earlier exhaustion forgotten, as she swept through the darkness like an arrow unbound, passing piles and piles of discarded necklaces, insigniae, and other items bearing the holy symbols of the gods, tossed to the side as garbage like their previous owners.
Nyla could hear Rozena shouting behind her, noticed incorporeal arms trying to hold her back, but ignored all of them until she reached the bridge. As she ran towards the first rope, her feet got caught in the ledge and she fell over, scraping her hands on the rough stone but could not pay it any mind as she got up and rushed towards the nearest rope.
Looking down, she could see the familiar robe of the Redeemer swaying beneath her, wrapped around the lanky body and almost bald head of Father Galvin, who had always scolded Nyla for lifting cupcakes off the tray, but had been patient whenever she had questions about anything. Now his eyes stared empty up at the sky, the mouth open in a silent, endless scream.
"Wait!" Nyla sobbed, her voice breaking up as she felt her eyes fill up with more than the rain, burning her cheeks. "Just hold on, I-... I'll get you up!"
She reached over the bridge railing, grabbing the tense rope and pulled with all her might, but she could only lift it up a few inches before it slipped out of her clam fingers and back into place. The corpse on the other hand did not make a sound in complaint.
"No no no no no," Nyla cried as she ran to the next rope that was bound around the bridge railing, her tears now washing away even the rain streaming down her face. A gust of wind blew her hood off, but she could not care less in the moment as she grabbed the rope, pulled, failed, yelled out in frustration and continued to the next, and the next, and the next, and the next.
"Pleaseeee! Please don't be dead! This is all a prank, right? Please, tell me that this isn't real!" Despite Nyla's cries, the darkness did not answer, and the rain swallowed her words.
Rozena flitted through the air behind her, desperate to offer comfort, help, or anything, but despite her tries, she could not even lift the hood back into place to shield Nyla from the rain.
Finally, as she had already arrived near the center of the bridge, Nyla broke down while lifting the rope holding Sister Marjerie, a nun from the temple of the Eternal Mother who had helped her adjust to her new body, back when she was still stumbling around like a newborn fawn on thin legs.
With a thumb, the rope snapped back into place as Nyla's knees gave out, pinning her fingers painfully between the tense rope and the stone. Nyla cried out in pain and frustration, before collapsing forward and hitting her head against the railing,
Why? Why why why was she so weak? If only she was stronger, she could have saved them... Perhaps in her old body, the wrong one, she would have possessed the strength to pull them up. Nyla had never once wished to return to those times, but... Did she truly deserve her happiness if she couldn't even save those she loved, those she depended on, those she still owed a debt to?
But... what good would that have done? They were already dead, weren't they? They'd been for who knows how long. If only...
"If only I had been there," she sobbed, trying to crouch down and huddle into a ball, but her hands were still pinned on top of the railing, forcing her to sit upright and lean against the cold stone.
"I could have helped them! Maybe! If I'd been there, maybe I could have saved them!" Nyla cried into the uncaring darkness, until she felt the presence of two familiar hands wrapping around her shoulders, and Rozena's voice next to her ear, her voice shaking, but comforting.
"I know... I know Honey. But... You could have done nothing better than what you did. This isn't your fault!"
"Even so!" Nyla sobbed, leaning back into Rozena's nonexistent touch. "I should have been with them! At least I... then I could have also-"
"Nyla!" Rozena's voice was stern, cutting through Nyla's pain like a hot knife and deep into her mind. "Don't talk like that... Please."
Nyla knelt stunned, staring down the bridge where she had come from, as the turmoil in her muddled mind cleared up and the full weight finally bore down on her. Her eyes filled up with tears again and she yanked her hands free from the rope, yelling out in pain as the stone violently scraped against her skin. Freed from the restraints, she was finally able to collapse fully, placing her forehead on the ground beneath. 
"I'm sorry!" She cried into the cobblestone, slowly rising into a scream. "I'm sorry, I'm sorry, I'm sorry!" 
She didn't even know who she was apologizing to. Rozena? The corpses that were dangling beneath her feet? Regardless, all she knew was that it felt good to release this pain, knowing that Rozena's hand rested on her back. After what felt like half an eternity, she had calmed down enough that her screams had turned into small sobs, and Rozena finally spoke.
"Come on, Honey... We should go. It's not good to remain any longer."
Nyla looked up at her, then shook her head, large droplets of water spraying in all directions. "No, I... I have to know. There could still be some alive, maybe they managed to lock themselves in the temple, barricade the door, something!" She searched for Rozena's eyes and found them, pleading with her eyes to allow her to go. Unsure, Rozena looked up the bridge, in the direction of the temples, then back at Nyla, her face full of pity, but without her usual conviction.
"I-... I don't know Honey. I don't think that-"
"But you don't know that! Right? You don't know that there's no one left!" Nyla's forceful plea caught Rozena off guard, and she nodded slowly.
"That is true, I suppose."
"Then I'll go. I need to do this."
With that, Nyla once again scrambled to her punished legs and broke into a sprint through the dark rains, towards the temple she called her home.
